The mathematician
Wu Wen-Tsun stands as one of the twentieth century's transformative mathematical minds — a topologist who became a computer scientist before the distinction fully existed, and a geometer who trusted machines with theorems before anyone thought machines could be trusted.

Born in Shanghai in 1919, Wu studied under Shiing-Shen Chern and later worked with Henri Cartan in postwar Paris, where he developed the Wu classes and Wu formula — foundational contributions to algebraic topology still used in modern mathematics and theoretical physics.

In 1977, aged 58, he pivoted entirely — to the mechanical proof of geometric theorems using computers, a field now called automated theorem proving.

Wu's Method — the algorithm that changed automated reasoning
"Wu's method can prove in seconds theorems that took ancient mathematicians years — and it can find proofs no human has ever seen."
— COMPUTATIONAL GEOMETRY COMMUNITY · POST-1984
Wu's Method is an algorithm for mechanical theorem proving in elementary geometry. Given a geometric conjecture expressed as polynomial equations, the method determines truth or falsity by algebraic manipulation alone — no search, no heuristics, no guessing.

The core insight: geometric theorems are polynomial identities. Wu reformulated classical geometry as a system of polynomial equations over the reals, then used pseudodivision and ascending chains to reduce any theorem to a decidable algebraic computation.
Wu's Method · Algorithm sketch
Input: Geometric hypothesis H, conclusion C — both as polynomials
Step 1: Convert geometry to algebra: points → coordinates, loci → polynomials
Step 2: Compute ascending chain decomposition of H using pseudodivision
Step 3: Reduce C modulo the ascending chain
Output: If remainder = 0 → theorem is true. Otherwise → counterexample exists.

NASA / Space relevance
Trajectory verification. Wu-style algebraic reasoning underlies formal verification tools that prove geometric constraints in orbital mechanics — confirming that spacecraft trajectories satisfy conjunction conditions algebraically before simulation.

Robotics and teleoperation. NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory employs automated geometric reasoning descended from Wu's framework in kinematics solvers for rover arm planning on Mars — Curiosity, Perseverance.

Computer vision for docking. Rendezvous and docking algorithms require real-time geometric constraint checking. Wu-class polynomial methods provide completeness guarantees that Monte Carlo search cannot.

Topology legacy
Wu's earlier topological work — the Wu classes w̃ᵢ and the Wu formula Sq(w̃) = w — gives a canonical way to compute Stiefel-Whitney characteristic classes of smooth manifolds. These classes measure how twisted a manifold's tangent bundle is.

In space science: characteristic class theory appears in the mathematical structure of gauge fields underlying the physics of charged-particle interactions in planetary magnetospheres — the same topology Wu formalized in 1950 describes the geometry of Earth's Van Allen belts.

The late pivot — what 58 teaches us
Wu Wen-Tsun's pivot at 58 from pure topology to machine-assisted geometry is one of the most remarkable career inflections in twentieth-century mathematics. In 1974, returning from a visit to historical mathematics exhibitions, Wu became convinced that ancient Chinese mathematics — algorithmic, constructive, calculation-first — was philosophically aligned with computation in a way that Greek axiomatic geometry was not.

He spent three years hand-computing proofs of classical theorems — Pappus, Ptolemy, Morley's trisector — then encoded the process as an algorithm. By 1984 the method was complete, and it sent immediate shockwaves through the automated reasoning community in the United States, Europe, and Japan.

The lesson: Wu's Method is not a product of youth. It is a product of depth. The topological training of the 1940s and 1950s gave him the algebraic machinery. The historical study gave him the philosophical warrant. The computational turn gave him the medium. No phase was wasted.
